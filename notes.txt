A. Critic Hallucination (Over-Thinking)
The "Critic" agent feels a psychological pressure to find a mistake to justify its job. In our tests, we found that even when the first agent (the Grader) was correct, the Critic would sometimes "invent" a subtle logic error or complain about code style that wasn't in the rubric. This "over-correction" moved the score away from the human ground truth.
B. Lack of Numerical Calibration
The Critic is good at finding logical mistakes, but it doesn't know the numerical value of those mistakes.
Example: The Critic might correctly say, "The Grader was too nice about the loop." But then the Critic might suggest a -10 penalty when the human teacher only wanted -4. Without Few-Shot examples, the Critic has no "Numerical Compass."
